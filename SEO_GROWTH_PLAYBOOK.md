# Slugsera Organic Search Growth Playbook

Last updated: 2026-08-25

## What this release fixes

- Every indexable route has a unique search title, description, canonical URL, social preview, visible H1, and crawl-time HTML file.
- Product canonicals and internal links use descriptive name slugs instead of inventory codes; old code URLs still resolve and redirect in the app.
- Category pages include useful buying context, crawlable product links, breadcrumbs, and `ItemList` schema.
- Product pages include `Product`, `Offer`, shipping, return-policy, and breadcrumb structured data.
- The XML sitemap includes public articles, descriptive product URLs, last-modified dates, and product-image entries.
- Homepage slides no longer download every hero image at once; below-fold editorial and product imagery is lazy decoded.

## Search Console release checklist

The Google verification tag must remain in `index.html`. Ownership itself can only be confirmed inside the verified Google Search Console property.

1. Open the `https://www.slugsera.com/` domain property and confirm ownership is green.
2. Submit `https://www.slugsera.com/sitemap.xml` once after this release.
3. Inspect the homepage, all three collection pages, two in-stock product pages, and the three newest articles.
4. Use **Test live URL**, confirm the canonical and rendered HTML, then request indexing.
5. Review Page indexing, Core Web Vitals, Merchant listings, Product snippets, Breadcrumbs, and HTTPS reports weekly for the first month.
6. Connect the live Merchant Center feed at `https://www.slugsera.com/merchant-feed.xml` and keep free listings enabled.

## Backlinks: quality rules

Do not buy bulk links, automate blog comments, exchange site-wide footer links, or publish spun guest posts. A backlink is useful when a relevant editor independently chooses to cite a genuinely helpful Slugsera page.

Priority opportunities:

1. Ask the K.R. Mangalam/Foundry portfolio team to link the existing Slugsera profile to the canonical homepage and founder-story article.
2. Maintain complete, consistent website links on Instagram, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile (if eligible), YouTube, Pinterest, and relevant founder profiles.
3. Pitch original data or expert commentary to Indian fashion, textile, student-founder, Delhi culture, sustainability, and D2C publications.
4. Offer photography plus a concise fact sheet for editorial coverage of small-batch production, fabric GSM, student entrepreneurship, and Delhi streetwear.
5. Collaborate with photographers, stylists, campus societies, artists, and slow-fashion creators on a real lookbook or event recap that both parties can reference.
6. Reclaim unlinked brand mentions by politely asking the publisher to link the words “Slugsera” or “Slug's Era” to the most relevant canonical page.

Keep every pitch personal, factual, and useful even if no link is added.

## Twelve-week publishing cadence

- Week 1: Refresh an existing product or collection page using Search Console queries and customer questions.
- Week 2: Publish one practical guide with original examples, measurements, or photography.
- Week 3: Distribute that guide through owned social channels and send 5–10 relevant, personal editorial pitches.
- Week 4: Update internal links, fix query cannibalisation, and report impressions, clicks, CTR, average position, indexed pages, rich-result validity, and assisted revenue.

Repeat the cycle around four topic clusters:

- Oversized T-shirt fit, GSM, cotton, sizing, styling, and care.
- Indian/Delhi streetwear outfits, seasons, creators, campuses, and culture.
- Slow-fashion production, cost per wear, small batches, sourcing, and durability.
- Slugsera products, artwork stories, founder journey, customer fits, drops, and lookbooks.

## Content quality gate

Every new article must answer one real search question, include first-hand Slugsera knowledge, link to at least two relevant internal pages, use original or properly licensed imagery, have one descriptive H1, and receive a unique title/description/sitemap entry. Do not publish merely to hit a word count or keyword frequency.
